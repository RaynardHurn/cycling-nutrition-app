*Your 90 g/h race target is appropriate only if you tolerate it. You have successfully used 90 g/h during a race, but sometimes experience bloating. Try 75–80 g/h first if the conditions or your stomach make 90 g/h difficult. Active recovery defaults to 0 g/h; eating beforehand or drinking carbohydrates is still reasonable when needed.

Simple fueling formulas

Carbohydrate during riding

Ride duration (hours) × on-bike carbs (g/h) = total grams to take with you.
Example: 1.5 h × 55 g/h = 82.5 g ≈ 83 g.

Daily carbohydrate

70 kg × recommended daily carbs (g/kg) = total daily carbs, including on-bike fuel. Subtract on-bike carbs for your meals and snacks.

TrainingPeaks kJ estimate

Average power (W) × duration (hours) × 3.6 = work (kJ). For 190 W × 1.5 h, this is 1,026 kJ.

Recovery foundation

Protein is useful on rest days too: aim around 105 g on rest/recovery days and 112–119 g on most training days at 70 kg. A hard training day can justify more. After training, plan roughly 25–35 g protein in your next meal.

The calculator uses your 70 kg reference. Energy expenditure is a broad approximation using 0.95–1.2 kcal per mechanical kJ and does not include your whole-day energy needs. The daily carbohydrate target remains editable; switching workout type or duration returns it to the recommended starting point.
